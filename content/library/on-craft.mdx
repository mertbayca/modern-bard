There's a particular satisfaction that comes from making something well. Not perfectly—perfection is an illusion—but *well*. With attention. With care.

## The similarity between code and prose

I've noticed something curious: the best code and the best prose share similar qualities. Both prize clarity. Both benefit from revision. Both require an understanding of rhythm and structure.

A well-crafted function, like a well-crafted sentence, does one thing beautifully. It doesn't meander. It doesn't apologize. It exists with purpose.

```typescript
// A function should read like prose
function calculateReadingTime(text: string): number {
  const wordsPerMinute = 200;
  const words = text.trim().split(/\s+/).length;
  return Math.ceil(words / wordsPerMinute);
}
```

Notice the clarity. The intention. The restraint.

## Craft as practice

Craft isn't something you achieve; it's something you practice. Every essay, every project, every line of code is an opportunity to hone your eye, to develop your taste, to understand what "good" means in your particular domain.

The modern bard knows this. They know that tools change—from quill to keyboard, from print to pixel—but the underlying principles of craft remain constant:

1. **Clarity over cleverness**
2. **Revision as refinement**
3. **Form following function**
4. **The right word in the right place**

## The long game

In an age of rapid iteration and "move fast and break things," craft feels almost subversive. It asks us to slow down. To care about details that most people won't notice.

But here's the secret: *you'll* notice. And over time, that attention to craft compounds. It becomes your signature, your voice, your contribution to the world.

So take your time. Revise. Refine. Make something worth making.
